    5. [Text book: Section 3.4.5]
Given functions f(n) and g(n) whose growth rates are expressed as algebraic equations, we might like to determine if one grows faster than the other. The best way to do this is to take the limit of the two functions as n grows towards infinity,

If the limit goes to ∞, then f(n) is in Ω(g(n)) because f(n) grows faster. If the limit goes to zero, then f(n) is in O(g(n)) because g(n) grows faster. If the limit goes to some constant other than zero, then f(n) = Ѳ(g(n)) because both grow at the same rate.
